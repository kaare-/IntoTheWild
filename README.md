# Villmarks

A small wilderness MUD. The valley is already there: a brook, a still pool, a slope, a rock overhang, dry wood and berries under that rock, a pot, poison berries, mushrooms, an open pile, one heavy log, one loose stone on the slope at (2,0), two lumps of clay on the bank at (2,2), and one deer on the trail. People and external agents use the same rules. Rain soaks whatever the sky can reach. Bark on a frame supported at both ends is a roof; a half-finished frame is not. The same bark, with a dry branch, is tinder for a fire.

The simulation is a Rust library with no network, database, clock, or model calls. One Tokio and Axum process owns the valley. A TypeScript page and a Python client are players, not special cases. Design choices taken from EmpireMUD, Bunnyland, Aardwolf, and GemStone III are in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md). Nothing here is deployed.

## Run it

Rust 1.99.1 is pinned in `rust-toolchain.toml`. You need Node and Python 3.

```bash
cargo build -p villmarks-server
cd web && npm install && npm run build && cd ..
cargo run -p villmarks-server
```

The valley listens on [http://127.0.0.1:18431](http://127.0.0.1:18431). The action window is 20 seconds (`config/evening.toml`). Open a second browser tab and take a different name. `sessionStorage` keeps each tab's token.

```bash
python3 agent/play.py --name Linde
```

That client uses a scripted policy and no model key. To let an external model pick from the same allowed actions, set `VILLMARKS_MODEL_URL`, `VILLMARKS_MODEL_NAME`, and `VILLMARKS_MODEL_KEY`. The world does not wait on the model.

Useful flags:

```bash
cargo run -p villmarks-server -- --help
cargo run -p villmarks-server -- --manual          # advance with POST /v1/advance
cargo run -p villmarks-server -- --window-secs 2   # shorter windows
cargo run -p villmarks-server -- --data data/valley.sqlite
```

Delete the sqlite file to reseed the valley. A living file keeps the tuning it was born with. The web dev server, if you want it, is `npm run dev` in `web/` on port 47321 and proxies the API.

Commands: `look`, `examine wood|bark|berries|poison berries|mushrooms|pot|mud|water|stone|clay|bowl|spear|carcass|fire|deer|tracks|log|frame|rock|slope|self|here|<name>`, `say`, `n` `s` `e` `w`, `gather branch|bark|berries|poison berries|mushrooms|pot|mud|water|stone|clay|bowl|spear|carcass`, `drop`, `grab`, `drag n|s|e|w`, `release`, `raise frame`, `lay bark`, `drink`, `boil`, `set clay`, `bind spear`, `hunt`, `light fire`, `cook`, `eat berries`, `eat carcass`, `smear mud`, `interrupt`, `help`.

One loose stone lies on the slope at (2,0). `gather stone` lifts it. From then on it is one carried thing with a stable id, and it is no longer on the ground. `drop stone` puts that same stone back where you stand. Someone outside sight does not see it. It is spoken as one stone: "A stone lies in contact with the ground here, in the open. The stone is dry. The sky is open above it. It is a found stone." In the arms, examine is one sentence: "A dry stone is carried by you, in the open, and it is a found stone." The id stays on the server. Branches and berries stay quantities.

A carried sheet of bark is one sentence: "A dry sheet of bark is carried by you, under the rock overhang." A carried pot is one sentence: "A dry pot is carried by you, under the rock overhang, and it can boil water over a fire." Dry sits inside the sentence. Look says "a dry sheet of bark" and "a dry pot". In the arms, one mushroom is "a dry mushroom" and two are "two dry mushrooms". A handful of berries is "a dry handful of berries". Examine of that carried food uses the same words: "Two dry mushrooms are carried by you, under the rock overhang. They must be cooked. Raw mushrooms do not feed you." and "A dry handful of berries is carried by you, under the rock overhang. They are good to eat raw." Dry sits inside the sentence. One stone stays "a dry stone". One pot stays "A dry pot is carried by you, …, and it can boil water over a fire." One spear stays "a spear with an unhardened point" or "a spear with a hardened point". One pot on the ground is a pot. Look names a single thing on the ground, including a pot just dropped ("A pot sits here.") and one sheet of bark ("A sheet of bark lies here."). A count of one is not called a pile. Two or more stay a pile, and look names that pile too ("A pile of bark lies here.", "A pile of branches lies here."). The map still draws one letter. When the cell holds something that letter does not show, the next mark is `+`. The prose is the list. Piles of branches and berries stay quantities.

Two lumps of clay lie on the bank at (2,2), on the valley floor beside the brook and the still pool. `gather clay` lifts one lump. `set clay` while you stand at a burning fire puts that lump on the ground. Four ticks later (`clay_ticks` in `config/evening.toml`), if the fire is still burning, the clay hardens into a bowl. While it is hardening, look says "A lump of clay is hardening in the fire." on every tick of that wait. The sentence stops when the bowl is there. `gather bowl` lifts it. `boil` with the bowl works the same as with the pot: the water is drunk, and the bowl is not used up. Unfired clay cannot boil water. If the fire is out when those four ticks are up, the clay stays soft. Someone outside sight does not see the clay. There is no kiln, no wheel, and no metal. The seeded pot stays under the rock.

`bind spear` spends one carried stone and one carried branch and leaves one spear in the arms. The stone and the branch are gone, and neither is copied onto the ground. The spear keeps one id. Holding that spear on a burning fire hardens the point after four ticks (`spear_ticks` in `config/evening.toml`). While the point is hardening, look says "The point of your spear is hardening in the fire." on every tick of that wait. The sentence stops when the point has hardened. The same id is still the spear. A nearby character reads "A spear with an unhardened point is carried by …" before that, and "A spear with a hardened point is carried by …" after. Look in the arms says "a spear with an unhardened point" or "a spear with a hardened point". On the ground, look says "A spear lies here. The point is not hardened." or "A spear lies here. The point is hardened." A spear left on the ground, or carried away from the fire, does not harden. A spear on the ground is the letter `j`.

`hunt` needs a hardened spear, and the deer on your cell or the next one. An unhardened spear is refused and does not wound the deer. On the deer's own cell the point brings it down. On the next cell the conditions are even enough for a recorded draw: the deer is killed, missed, or bolts. The same seed repeats that draw. A miss or a bolt leaves the deer alive and moved. A kill leaves the deer gone and one carcass on that cell. `gather carcass` lifts it. `eat carcass` is refused: "The carcass must be cooked." The carcass stays in the arms, and the hunger does not ease. `cook` at a fire is what turns the carcass into food. The result says the hunger word the body already uses. When the energy word also changes, it says that too: rested, tiring, or spent. When the energy word stays, it does not add an energy line. Someone who cannot see the deer does not see the kill. The spear is not used up.

Thirst rises every tick, whether or not anyone is here. Rain wets clothes and does not quench it. `drink` works in the brook or in the still pool at (2,1). A raw drink eases thirst and rolls a recorded draw. Running water, the brook, turns your stomach only occasionally (two times in ten, in this valley). Still water does it almost every time (nine times in ten). The illness amount stays on the server. You hear "well" or "ill". It fades on its own, a little every tick, until you are well again. `gather water` at either source, carry it to a fire, and `boil` it. Boiling needs the pot that sits with the supplies under the rock, or a bowl fired from the clay. The pot is not used up, and neither is the bowl. Unfired clay cannot boil water. The boiled drink eases thirst and does not roll that draw. It also does not cure an illness you already have. Hunger rises the same way thirst does. Energy does too: "rested", "tiring", or "spent". Bark is tinder. `light fire` spends one dry sheet of bark and one dry branch from the arms when those are carried, and from the pile at the feet only when the arms do not already hold that fuel. The line says which one was used. Rain puts that fire out in the open; the rock overhang or a bark roof keeps it. Sweet berries, the pile under the rock, can be eaten raw and give energy. They do not ease hunger. The handfuls at (4,1) are poisonous. Eating them turns your stomach and does not feed you, and cooking them does not make them food. Mushrooms at (3,1) must be heat-treated; raw mushrooms are refused and stay where they were. `cook` works only while a fire is burning where you stand, and it takes a mushroom first, then sweet berries, from your arms or from the ground at your feet. Bark is still tinder, not food. One raw drink, one boiled drink, one raw handful of sweet berries, or one cooked portion takes the edge off. Those amounts stay on the server. You hear "not thirsty", "thirsty", or "parched", and "not hungry", "hungry", or "starving".

A deer is already on the trail at (2,3). It walks one cell every tick, including when nobody has sent a command, along the trail and then to the berries under the rock. If it is standing on a pile of berries it eats one handful and stays for that tick. The handful leaves the pile. It is not a new item. The earlier handfuls are "A deer eats a handful of berries." The last one is "A deer eats the last handful of berries." While the deer is on the pile and eating, look says "A deer is eating the berries here." It does not also say berries lie here and a deer stands here. When the pile is gone, look does not say berries still lie there. Someone who cannot see that cell does not get the line. While the deer is eating, `examine berries` says "A deer is eating them." One handful is "A handful of berries", not a pile. When the deer is not eating them, the deer is not added. Someone who cannot see that cell still hears "You see no berries from here." Someone standing where the deer ate the last handful, while that deer is still on the cell, hears "The berries that were here are gone. The deer here ate them." Once that deer has left, it is "The berries that were here are gone. A deer ate them." If the deer did not eat them, the sentence stays "The berries that were here are gone." Someone who cannot see that cell still hears "You see no berries from here." While that deer is still on the cell, `examine deer` adds "It ate the berries here." From the next cell it is "It ate the berries there." Once the deer has left, that sentence is not added. A deer that did not eat them is not described as having eaten. Someone who cannot see the deer cannot examine it. The pile is not put back. The cell it walks off keeps hoofprints, and those prints stay. `examine tracks` names each visible mark once, by how far it lies, so two marks that share a direction are not the same sentence twice. Marks out of sight stay out. `examine deer` places the deer the same way: how many cells south, north, east, or west. "To the south and west" is not enough when more than one cell lies that way. Someone who cannot see the deer cannot examine it. `examine deer` and `examine tracks` are the same examine command as the wood and the fire. There is no separate animal command. A person sees the deer, the prints, and what it just did only inside their own sight. Someone who arrives later can still find the prints.

The wet season arrives at tick 18, while the rain is already falling, whether or not anyone has sent a command. From then on, rain in the open soaks wood twice as fast. The rock overhang and a bark roof keep the same shelter. Nothing dries. Someone who can see that open wood reads "The rain is heavier on the wood this season." Someone who cannot see it does not get that line, or the event on that ground. The commands stay the same.

Loose branches left in the open rot. Every 8 ticks one of them is gone, with or without a command. The line is "Wood left in the open is rotting one cell south and one cell west, in the open." from the person looking, the same cell count `examine wood` uses. Someone who cannot see that wood does not get the line. When one goes, the event says where, in cells from the hearer, the same way tracks do: "A branch rots away one cell north and one cell east, in the open. You hear branches breaking." "You hear branches breaking." stays with that place. Someone who cannot hear it does not get the line. The pile stays where it was. `examine wood` uses that same cell count from the person looking. "To the north and east" is not enough when that names the neighboring diagonal instead of the pile. Someone who cannot see the wood cannot examine it. A rock overhang or a bark roof stops that, because the sky is no longer open on that cell. A branch built into a frame is not loose, so it is not in the rot. Bark, berries, mushrooms, and the log are not this rot. Someone who cannot see the open wood does not get the line, and does not hear the break.

A nearby character also hears "You hear branches breaking." when the deer steps through loose branches, and when someone gathers a branch. A deer step on empty trail is quiet, and so is eating berries or lighting a fire. The sentence is part of the event that already happened. Sight is still two cells. A distant character does not get it.

A body in the open gets cold, and the wind is on them. The same exposure the rain already uses decides it: the rock overhang stops it, a bark roof lets a little through, open sky lets the wind double the cold. A body standing on a burning fire warms, and that tick does not also add the sky's cold. For a few ticks after they step off, or after the fire goes out, the sky's cold is halved, and the wind still cools them. While that leftover is what keeps the word warm, the look says so, and the sentence goes when the word leaves warm. The next cell does not warm. Someone standing there reads that the heat does not reach them. Rain still puts an open fire out. The sun on that body is unchanged. The sun burns bare skin while the rain is still off. `gather mud` at the brook or the still pool, then `smear mud`. A smear leaves a quarter of that sun. Mud flakes off, and rain in the open washes it off. The words are "warm", "chilly", or "cold"; "out of the wind", "in a little wind", or "in the wind"; "unburned", "pink", or "burned"; "bare skin", "thin mud", or "mud-smeared". `examine rock` describes the overhang when you can see it. Under it, the sentence is "Rock leans overhead. You are out of the wind. Rain does not reach you." From a cell that can see it, the same shelter is said of the rock. Someone who cannot see the overhang cannot examine it. `examine slope` describes the slope when you can see it: "The slope is open ground. It runs north from where you stand." Someone who cannot see that slope cannot examine it. From tick 12 the still pool is frozen unless a fire burns on it. The brook keeps running. The amounts stay on the server.

## An evening

Rain starts at tick 16. One person can still roof a frame before that if they take three branches and bark from under the rock and build on open ground. The open pile at (5,2) and the dry pile under the rock are the comparison. Two people can drag the log; one cannot.

This session was played through the player API against `villmarks-server --manual` (three clients, then a fourth who walked out of sight). Sera and Otto took the log. Linde built the roof. The interrupted drag did not move the log; the next one did.

```text
t7  The heavy log is in contact with the ground here, in the open. It is dry.
    Otto and Sera are gripping it.
t14 A frame stands here, under a bark roof. Supports: 2. It has a beam.
    It is supported at both ends. Bark lies over it and is dry.
    The wood in the frame is dry. Only a little rain reaches it.
```

After the rain, Linde examined the wood:

```text
A pile of 1 branch is in contact with the ground to the east, under the rock
overhang. The wood is dry. Rain does not reach it.
A pile of 4 branches is in contact with the ground to the south, in the rain.
The wood is soaked. Rain reaches it.
```

The frame's own wood was damp, under soaked bark: wet bark is a worse roof than dry bark, and it is still less than open sky. Nils, standing at the west edge, got `You see no wood from here.` His map had no wood glyph.

Sera's map after the rain:
