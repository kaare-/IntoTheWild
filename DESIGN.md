# Design notes

Villmarks is one small valley and one authoritative process. The simulation crate has no network, database, wall clock, or model calls. The server batches intents and steps the core. Browser and Python clients share that API.

## Cell, stock, and construction

EmpireMUD 2.0 beta (`EmpireMUD/EmpireMUD-2.0-Beta`) stores a player landscape change as a new sector. `ACMD(do_lay)` in `src/building.c` checks that the room can take a road, spends resources, and calls `change_terrain`. `change_terrain` in `src/db.world.c` calls `perform_change_sect`, which writes both `SECT(room)` and `world_map[x][y].sector_type` and marks the map dirty. `show_map_to_char` in `src/mapview.c` then draws a glyph from the sector icon, with road, building, and crop icons able to replace it. The command, the stored terrain, and the glyph are one chain.

That chain is the wrong split for this valley. Bark on a frame is not a new kind of ground. A cell keeps its region (slope, brook, floor) and its rock overhang. Loose wood is a quantity until someone lifts it or builds with it, and then it is an item with a stable id. A frame is a third thing: supports, a beam, and optional bark, sitting on a cell. A fire is a fourth: its own id, the cell it burns on, and the moment it goes out. Lighting one spends a dry sheet of bark and a dry branch once, from the arms when those are carried and from the pile at the feet only otherwise. The line says which. The fire is not a second copy of that wood, and bark already laid as a roof is not tinder. The map glyph is computed from what that person can see. A half-finished frame stays on the cell and does not become a terrain type. Material is counted once: pile count plus item count. Berries are a pile like branches. One loose stone lies on the slope at (2,0). Gathering it mints an item id, and dropping it puts that same item back on the ground. A single stone is spoken as one stone, not as a pile count. The id stays off the observation. A carried sheet of bark and a carried pot are each one sentence, with dry inside it, and one pot is a pot. In the arms, two mushrooms are two dry mushrooms, and a handful of berries is a dry handful of berries. Examine of that carried food uses those same words, says where they are, and still says whether they must be cooked or can be eaten raw. `examine rock` describes the overhang when it is in sight. Under it, you are out of the wind and rain does not reach you. Out of sight, you cannot examine it. `examine slope` describes the slope when it is in sight: open ground, and which way it runs from where you stand. Out of sight, you cannot examine it. One stone, one pot, and one spear stay the sentences they already have. Look names a single thing on the ground, and a count of one is not called a pile. Two or more stay a pile. Look names every kind on the cell, including a pile of bark. The map keeps one glyph and adds `+` when that cell holds something the glyph does not show. Branches and berries stay quantities. Two lumps of clay lie on the bank at (2,2). A lump set at a burning fire hardens after `clay_ticks` (4) into a bowl that boils water the way the pot does. The pot stays. Unfired clay cannot boil. If the fire is out, the clay stays soft. There is no kiln. `bind spear` spends one carried stone and one carried branch and mints one spear item. Holding that spear on a burning fire hardens the point after `spear_ticks` (4). While that wait is running, look says "The point of your spear is hardening in the fire." A lump set by the fire says "A lump of clay is hardening in the fire." Each sentence stops when that thing has hardened. Neither sentence counts the ticks. The id does not change. A nearby character reads an unhardened point and a hardened point as different sentences. A spear on the ground is `j`. `hunt` needs a hardened spear and the deer on that cell or the next. The same cell is a kill. The next cell is even enough for a recorded draw, and the same seed repeats it. A miss or a bolt leaves the deer alive and moved. A kill removes the deer and leaves one carcass. `eat carcass` is refused with "The carcass must be cooked." The carcass stays, and the hunger does not ease. Cooking it on a fire is what makes it food. The result says the hunger word. When the energy word also changes, it says that word too. When the energy word stays, it does not invent an energy line. Someone outside sight does not see the kill. Cooking a handful over a burning fire is what eases hunger. Raw berries do not, and neither does the bark.

A deer is a fifth thing: one id, the cell it stands on, and an index on a fixed route. It is not a sector and not a second berry pile. Each tick it walks one cell, or, when berries are on the cell under its feet, eats one handful from that pile and stays. When that step lands on a pile of loose branches, one branch from that pile snaps and the count goes down by one. The line says a branch snapped under the deer, and where, in cells from the hearer. A step that does not land on a pile does not say branches are breaking and does not change a pile. It does not snap more than one branch per step. Someone who cannot see that step does not get the line. The last handful says it was the last one. While the deer is on that pile and eating, look says the deer is eating the berries, not berries lying here and a deer standing here as two facts. When the pile is gone, look does not say berries still lie there. Someone who cannot see that cell does not get the line. `examine berries` while the deer is eating says the deer is eating them. One handful is not called a pile. When the deer is not eating them, the deer is not added. Someone who cannot see that cell still hears that they see no berries from here. After that pile is gone, someone standing on the cell who examines berries hears that they are gone. When the deer that ate the last handful is still on that cell, the sentence says the deer here ate them. Once that deer has left, it says a deer ate them. When the deer did not eat them, it does not name a deer. Someone who cannot see that cell still hears that they see no berries from here. The pile is not put back. Eating uses the pile count only. The cell it leaves keeps hoofprints. Those marks are a set of cells, not a terrain change, and they do not decay in this valley yet. `examine tracks` names each visible mark once, by how far it lies. Marks out of sight stay out. `examine deer` uses that same count of cells. While the deer that ate the last handful is still on that cell, `examine deer` says it ate the berries there. Once it has left, that sentence is not added. A deer that did not eat them is not described as having eaten. Out of sight, you cannot examine the deer. The glyph is `d` for the deer, ahead of wood and berries and behind a fire, and `"` for hoofprints when the cell would otherwise show only ground. Look names a deer on any cell in sight. A diagonal is not left unnamed because it is not due west or due south. When the deer is standing on the loose branches, that is one sentence with the cell count, not a wood line and a deer line that name the same cell. `examine deer` uses that same sentence while the deer is on the wood. When the deer has stepped off, the wood leaves the deer sentence. When hoofprints are on that same wood, the look line that names it also says they are there. When that wood has no hoofprints, the line does not add them. Someone who cannot see the cell does not get the line. When the deer is not on the wood, the two facts stay apart. Someone who cannot see that cell does not get the deer, and cannot examine it. A person sees the deer, the prints, and those events only inside their sight.

The wet season is one later tick, not a new kind of ground. Once it has fired, open rain uses twice the material and body gain. Exposure is unchanged, so a rock overhang and a bark roof still shed what they shed, and a bark sheet on a frame still takes the ordinary rain. Wood does not dry. The sentence and the event are cell-local: a person sees "The rain is heavier on the wood this season" only when open wood is inside their sight.

Loose branches on open ground rot. Every `rot_ticks` (8) one branch leaves a pile, or one loose branch lying on that ground. The test is exposure: under 1000, the rock and a bark roof, the branch stays. A branch whose role is a frame support or beam is not loose, so the frame is outside this rot. Bark, berries, the log, and hoofprints are outside it too. The sentence and the event use the same sight as the wood itself. The rot event says where, in cells from the hearer, the same way tracks do, and "You hear branches breaking." stays with that place. Someone who cannot hear it does not get the line. The pile stays where it was. `examine wood` uses that same cell count from the person looking. When hoofprints are on that wood, `examine wood` says so, with that same cell count. When that wood has no hoofprints, they are not added. When a deer is standing on the wood you examine, `examine wood` says so. If it is eating berries there, it says that. If it is only standing on the rotting branches, it says that. On the tick the deer steps onto the branches and a branch snaps, before a handful is gone, it says the deer is standing on these branches. It does not say the deer is eating until a handful is gone on that tick. When a handful does go, it says the deer is eating and that a handful is gone. If that handful is the last, it says so. On a tick when no handful goes, it does not say one is gone. On the tick the deer arrives and a branch snaps, look says the deer stands on these branches. It does not say the deer is eating until a handful is gone on that tick. The first time a handful goes and more than one handful is still on the cell, look says a handful is gone and that more are left. When a later handful goes and more than one still remain, look says another handful is gone, and that more are left. It does not repeat the first sentence. When exactly one handful remains, look says this handful is the last, and it does not wait until the pile hits 0. Those are not the same sentence. If the pile is already empty and the deer is still on the cell, look does not say the deer is eating. It still says the last handful is gone, and it says the deer stands here. Once the deer has left, it does not say the deer stands here. While that deer is still in sight, look says it stands there, placed the same way. When that deer is out of sight, the standing sentence is dropped. While a handful remains and the deer is eating, look keeps the eating sentence. After that, look on that cell says the berries that were here are gone. While the deer is still on the cell, it says the deer here ate them. After the deer has left that cell but is still in sight, look names that deer the way the rest of the look already places it, such as the deer one cell south and one cell west ate them, When that look already says the deer stands on the rotting wood, it does not repeat the count. It says the deer on that rotting wood stands there. When no other line has counted the cell, it still places the deer, such as one cell west. When that deer is no longer in sight, it says a deer ate them and does not say the deer stands. It does not say the deer is still here or still eating. The berries are not put back. Someone who cannot see that cell does not get the line. On a tick when no handful goes, look does not say one is gone. When no deer is on that wood, one is not added. Someone who cannot see the wood still cannot examine it. "To the north and east" is not enough when that names the neighboring diagonal instead of the pile. Someone who cannot see the wood cannot examine it. Look's "Wood left in the open is rotting" uses that same cell count, and "in the open" appears once. Someone who cannot see that wood does not get the line.

## One observation, two kinds of client

Bunnyland (the public how-it-works notes, client guide, and `thalismind/bunnyland-server` spec) puts human players and autonomous characters in the same rooms, with the same verbs and the same validation. Clients differ in presentation. An LLM is a controller for a character, not the world. Perception is character-scoped.

`World::observe` is that projection: prose, a fixed-width ASCII map, and JSON, including `allowed_actions`. Moisture numbers, tokens, hidden cells, and the cause log stay on the server. The Python client and the browser both call `POST /v1/intent` and `GET /v1/observe`. An optional model is configured with `VILLMARKS_MODEL_URL`, `VILLMARKS_MODEL_NAME`, `VILLMARKS_MODEL_KEY`, and `VILLMARKS_MODEL_TIMEOUT`. Its latency is only in the client. If the model is unset or fails, the scripted policy chooses. The server keeps sealing windows either way.

Bunnyland validates and commits per command. Villmarks collects every intent that arrived for the open window, sorts them by actor id and then command id, and commits one outcome. Receive order inside the window does not pick a winner.

## What the load test is

Aardwolf's public stats page, read on 2 November 2026, showed a most-online-since-reboot figure of 397 and an all-time figure of 853. Those reboot numbers move. GemStone III's published Simutronics timeline (Elonka Dunin) records more than 2000 simultaneous users in December 1996. Those are networked, long-lived games. They are context, not a target and not code we copied.

`cargo run --release -p villmarks-server --bin loadtest` times `World::step` for 20 windows. It does not open sockets, parse HTTP, or send WebSocket frames. The `sim+sqlite` rows also commit one SQLite transaction per window. Intent setup is outside the timer. Rain is disabled for the sample (`rain_starts_at = 10000`). The numbers are one machine, one run, not a capacity claim.

## Time and commands

Real time, simulation time, and the action window are separate. A window is 20 seconds unless `--window-secs` or `--manual` says otherwise. Manual play advances only on `POST /v1/advance`. The core itself never sleeps.

A command is an attempt. The core decides range, duration, and result. Look, examine, and help answer immediately and do not step the world. Anything that changes the valley is queued. A late hint (a client window number older than the current tick) still joins the open window; it is not dropped and it is not applied to a closed one. The same command id returns the stored result, including after a restart. A queued id that has not been sealed returns "Already queued". A parse failure is remembered for that id; send a new id to correct it.

One body attempt per actor per window (move, gather, drop, grab, drag, raise frame, lay bark, drink, boil, light a fire, cook, eat, smear mud). Say, interrupt, and release do not take that slot. Looking at the deer, its tracks, the pot, or the mushrooms is examine, not a new body attempt. Long work occupies the body until it finishes, is interrupted, or a precondition fails. Thirst, hunger, energy, illness, the deer, the wet season, and rot all move on the schedule even when the window was empty. A gather in that window is resolved before the deer eats, so a person and the deer do not both take the same handful. A raw drink eases thirst and records a draw (`contest_hash` with the tag `drink`). The subject ends in `:brook` or `:still`. The brook uses `illness_chance` (200 of 1000 here). The still pool at (2,1) uses `still_chance` (900 of 1000). When the draw falls under that chance, the illness amount jumps and the word becomes "ill". Each later tick lowers that amount until the word is "well". Boiling needs a carried pot and a burning fire. It consumes the water, not the pot, eases thirst, and records no illness draw. The boiled drink is not the cure. Sweet berries eaten raw lower energy only. Poison berries are eaten if someone chooses them: they are consumed, they do not feed, and the illness jumps the way a bad drink does. Cooking does not treat them as food. Mushrooms are still refused raw and are not consumed. Cooking prefers a mushroom, then sweet berries, and lowers both hunger and energy. Cold and sunburn rise with exposure every tick. A body on a burning fire loses `fire_warmth` that tick instead of taking the sky's cold, and keeps `warmth_hold` ticks of leftover heat after it steps off or the fire goes out. During those ticks the sky's cold is halved, so open wind still cools. The sentence "The warmth of the fire is still on you." stays on every later tick while that leftover is what keeps the word warm, including the open-wind ticks after the counter runs out, and it goes when the word leaves warm. Under a rock, once the counter is spent, the rock is what keeps the word warm and the sentence goes. Someone on the next cell does not warm, and their look says the heat does not reach them. Rain still puts an open fire out. Sunburn is not part of that relief. Open sky doubles the cold because the wind is there. A rock overhang stops both. A bark roof lets a little through. Mud smeared on skin quarters the sun while the smear lasts. Rain hides the sun and washes mud off open skin. From `chill_at` the still pool is frozen unless a fire is on that cell. The brook does not freeze. Energy uses the hunger tick, thresholds, and relief, and the client hears "rested", "tiring", or "spent". Two people pulling the same way can move the log. One person cannot. Two people grabbing one loose branch produce one item; the draw is `contest_hash(seed, time, tag, subject, actor)` and the hash is stored on the event. Two people lighting the same cell get one fire. A branch rotting, a deer stepping into or out of loose branches, and a successful gather of a branch append "You hear branches breaking." to that cell-scoped event. Empty-trail deer steps, the deer eating, and lighting a fire do not. Empty windows still rain, fade an illness, burn a fire down, walk the deer, turn the season, rot a branch left in the open, raise thirst, hunger, and energy, and finish work, including with nobody present. Rain in the open puts a fire out. A rock overhang or a bark roof does not.

## Log and persistence

Events have an id, a simulation time, affected objects, and cause ids when a prior event contributed. A log shift cites the grips and the drag. Independent events may share a tick. Ties break by event sequence. Draws are stored so a run can be replayed. The client event list has id, time, and text only.

SQLite is WAL, one connection, one transaction per join and per sealed window. A snapshot is written every `snapshot_every` records (5 by default) and again on SIGINT. Restore is the latest snapshot plus later log rows. The snapshot includes the clock, grips, work, the future schedule, and the seed. Queued intents that were not sealed are gone; resending them runs them once.
